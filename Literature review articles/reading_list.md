# Verified Reading List

**For:** MSc Thesis - AI-Based Multimodal Deep Learning Early Warning System for Drought, Crop Advisory, and Food Insecurity Prediction Using Satellite Imagery, Climate, Vegetation, Socioeconomic, and Agricultural Data in Eastern Ethiopia (Eastern Hararghe)

**Researcher:** Bona Nasser Abdo, MSc Data Science, Haramaya University

All entries were retrieved directly from the academic index during compilation; titles, authors, years and DOIs are copied from the retrieval results. Where the index returned no DOI, the venue is given instead.


## 1. Ethiopia and Horn of Africa: Drought, Agriculture and Food-Security Context

1. **Drought risk assessment and mapping under changing climate: the East Hararge Zone in Ethiopia** (2025). Abdulkerim Bedewi Serur, Huseyidin Mohammed Kebira. *Journal of Water and Climate Change*. https://doi.org/10.2166/wcc.2025.602
   Relevance: Directly maps drought risk in the thesis study area (East Hararghe) using CHIRPS rainfall, MODIS NDVI and CMIP6 projections, validating indices against crop yield anomalies (r = 0.72-0.78).

2. **Mapping the spatial and temporal variation of agricultural and meteorological drought using geospatial techniques, Ethiopia** (2020). Abebe Senamaw, Solomon Addisu, K. V. Suryabhagavan. *Environmental Systems Research*. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40068-020-00204-2
   Relevance: Provides the benchmark eMODIS NDVI/VCI and SPI drought risk framework with rainfall-NDVI and index-crop yield correlations (r = 0.71-0.74).

3. **Spatio-temporal Assessment of Drought in Ethiopia and the Impact of Recent Intense Droughts** (2019). Yuei-An Liou, Getachew Mulualem. *Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11151828
   Relevance: Countrywide MODIS NDVI, CHIRPS, soil moisture and LST analysis identifying the 2009 and 2015 El Nino droughts.

4. **Evaluation of Satellite-Based Rainfall Estimates and Application to Monitor Meteorological Drought for the Upper Blue Nile Basin, Ethiopia** (2017). Yared Bayissa, Tsegaye Tadesse, Getachew B. Demisse, et al.. *Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs9070669
   Relevance: Evaluates CHIRPS and other satellite rainfall products against gauges, establishing gridded rainfall reliability for the thesis's climate inputs.

5. **Developing a satellite-based combined drought indicator to monitor agricultural drought: a case study for Ethiopia** (2018). Yared Bayissa, Tsegaye Tadesse, Mark Svoboda, et al.. *GIScience & Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.1080/15481603.2018.1552508
   Relevance: Builds a PCA-weighted combined drought indicator (SPI, LST, NDVI, soil moisture) validated against crop yields, a precursor to multimodal index fusion.

6. **Governing agricultural drought: Monitoring using the vegetation condition index** (2016). Tagel Gebrehiwot, A. Veen, B. Maathuis. *Ethiopian Journal of Environmental Studies and Management*. https://doi.org/10.4314/ejesm.v9i3.9
   Relevance: Maps nationwide agricultural drought seasons from SPOT NDVI time series (1998-2013), identifying regions recurrently exposed to drought including eastern Ethiopia.

7. **Spatio-temporal assessment of agricultural drought using remote sensing and ground-based data indices in the Northern Ethiopian Highland** (2024). Kassahun Tenebo Alito, Mulu Sewinet Kerebih. *Journal of Hydrology: Regional Studies*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrh.2024.101700
   Relevance: Validates multi-index remote sensing drought assessment (NDVI, LST, VCI, VHI, SPI) against in-situ data, a template for satellite-ground validation.

8. **Trends and spatiotemporal patterns of meteorological drought incidence in North Wollo, northeastern highlands of Ethiopia** (2022). S. Wassie, D. Mengistu, A. B. Birlie. *Arabian Journal of Geosciences*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12517-022-10423-9
   Relevance: Combines CHIRPS-based Z-score, MODIS LST and Mann-Kendall trends to rank drought risk districts.

9. **Multiple Indices Based Agricultural Drought Assessment in the Rift Valley Region of Ethiopia** (2022). Bayisa Negasa Wolteji, Sintayehu Teka Bedhadha, Sintayehu Legese Gebre, et al.. *Environmental Challenges*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envc.2022.100488
   Relevance: Uses NDVI, LST, VCI, TCI and VHI to map agricultural drought severity across agroecologies in Oromia's Rift Valley.

10. **Developing earth observation-based combined drought indicator to monitor agricultural drought in Ethiopia** (2025). Zerihun Chere. *Journal of Hydrology: Regional Studies*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrh.2025.102556
   Relevance: Five-input combined agricultural drought index (precipitation, VCI, evapotranspiration, soil moisture, temperature) correlated with crop yields up to r = 0.87.

11. **Assessing the spatio-temporal variability of NDVI and VCI as indices of crops productivity in Ethiopia: a remote sensing approach** (2021). Jean Moussa Kourouma, Emmanuel Eze, Emnet Negash, et al.. *Geomatics, Natural Hazards and Risk*. https://doi.org/10.1080/19475705.2021.1976849
   Relevance: Quantifies how NDVI/VCI explain teff, maize and other crop yield variability in Ethiopia.

12. **Predictors of drought-induced crop yield/losses in two agroecologies of southern Tigray, Northern Ethiopia** (2022). Emmanuel Eze, Atkilt Girma, Amanuel Zenebe, et al.. *Scientific Reports*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-09862-x
   Relevance: Identifies rainfall and NDVI-derived predictors of crop losses across agroecologies.

13. **Evaluating a satellite-based seasonal evapotranspiration product and identifying its relationship with other satellite-derived products and crop yield: A case study for Ethiopia** (2015). Tsegaye Tadesse, G. B. Senay, Getachew Berhan, et al.. *International Journal of Applied Earth Observation and Geoinformation*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2015.03.006
   Relevance: Foundational study linking satellite evapotranspiration, NDVI, rainfall estimates and cereal yields across 41 Ethiopian cropping zones for food security early warning.

14. **Impacts of Rainfall Variability on Potato Productivity in Haramaya District, Eastern Hararge Zone, Ethiopia** (2021). Shame Ahmed Adem. *Journal of Chemical, Environmental and Biological Engineering*. https://doi.org/10.11648/j.jcebe.20210501.13
   Relevance: Documents a significant rainfall-potato yield correlation (r = 0.668) and farmer adaptation in Haramaya district within the study zone.

15. **Effect of climate variability on crop income and indigenous adaptation strategies of households** (2017). Arega Shumetie, Molla Alemayehu Yismaw. *Greater South Information System*. https://doi.org/10.60692/3fahf-b8315
   Relevance: Shows cropping-season rainfall variability significantly reduces smallholder crop income in moisture-stressed West Hararghe districts.

16. **Determining food intake, the requirement, and weighing the relative risk between food secure and insecure households in rural areas of Ethiopia: The case of West Hararghe zone** (2024). Firew Hailemariam, Kazi Md. Abir Aman. *African Journal of Food, Agriculture, Nutrition and Development*. https://doi.org/10.18697/ajfand.135.23020
   Relevance: Measures household food insecurity and its dependence on seasonal rainfall in West Hararghe.

17. **Seasonal and annual rainfall variability over East Hararghe Zone, Oromia, Ethiopia** (2025). Asalfew Negussie Cheru, Tesfaye Dessu Geleta, Gezahegn Mergia Tullu. *Academia Environmental Sciences and Sustainability*. https://doi.org/10.20935/acadenvsci7713
   Relevance: Characterizes Belg and Kiremt rainfall variability and ENSO-linked anomalies in East Hararghe (1981-2023).

18. **Analysis of Observed and Projected Climate Change and Variability in Wheat Production Belts of Southeastern Oromia, Ethiopia** (2025). Yared Tesfaye, Nigussie Dechassa, Yibekal Alemayehu, et al.. *International Journal of Agronomy*. https://doi.org/10.1155/ioa/7226297
   Relevance: Quantifies rainfall variability, drought recurrence and projected climate change risks to rainfed crop production in Oromia.

19. **Dry spell length analysis for crop production using Markov-Chain model in Eastern Hararghe, Ethiopia** (2017). Eba Muluneh Sorecha, Birhanu Bayissa. *Journal of Degraded and Mining Lands Management*. https://doi.org/10.15243/jdmlm.2017.044.891
   Relevance: Analyzes within-season dry spell probabilities in Babile, Haramaya and Kersa districts of the study area.

20. **Climate variability and change in the Central Rift Valley of Ethiopia: challenges for rainfed crop production** (2013). Belay T. Kassie, Reimund P. Rötter, H. Hengsdijk, et al.. *The Journal of Agricultural Science*. https://doi.org/10.1017/s0021859612000986
   Relevance: Foundational Ethiopian study linking rainfall variability, growing season length and warming to rainfed crop risk.

21. **Understanding climate as a driver of food insecurity in Ethiopia** (2017). Kirsty Lewis. *Climatic Change*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10584-017-2036-7
   Relevance: Argues that localized rainfall variability in marginal eastern livelihood zones, not national totals, drives Ethiopian food insecurity.

22. **Impact and adaptation of climate variability and change on small-holders and agriculture in Ethiopia: A review** (2023). Mulat Shibabaw Demem. *Heliyon*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e18972
   Relevance: Comprehensive review of climate impacts on Ethiopian crops, livestock and food security with adaptation evidence from Oromia, Somali and Tigray.

23. **Harnessing climate smart agriculture across the maize value chain in southern Ethiopia: A multilevel analysis of climate risks and adaptation strategies** (2026). Dejene W. Sintayehu, Sintayehu Alemayehu, D. Lule, et al.. *Climate Smart Agriculture*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csag.2026.100112
   Relevance: Assesses climate risks and climate-smart agriculture adoption along the Ethiopian maize value chain.

24. **Human-induced climate change increased 2021-2022 drought severity in horn of Africa** (2025). Joyce Kimutai, Clair Barnes, Mariam Zachariah, et al.. *Weather and Climate Extremes*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wace.2025.100745
   Relevance: Attribution analysis of the worst drought in 40 years across southern Ethiopia, Somalia and Kenya.

25. **Monitoring Extreme Agricultural Drought over the Horn of Africa (HOA) Using Remote Sensing Measurements** (2019). Carolyn Qu, Xianjun Hao, John J. Qu. *Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11080902
   Relevance: Tracks long-term vegetation health decline and 2015-2016 El Nino drought across the Horn of Africa from MODIS VHI/TCI.

26. **Drought Dynamics From Meteorological Stress to Agricultural Impacts Using Physically-Based Remote Sensing Indices in the Horn of Africa** (2025). N. K. Ahmed, Shuanggen Jin. *International Journal of Climatology*. https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.70178
   Relevance: Introduces drought propagation and crop stress indices showing 30-35% production losses in Somalia and drought hotspots in eastern Ethiopia.

27. **Drought vulnerability and impacts of climate change on livestock production and productivity in different agro-Ecological zones of Ethiopia** (2022). Girma Asefa Bogale, Zelalem Bekeko. *Journal of Applied Animal Research*. https://doi.org/10.1080/09712119.2022.2103563
   Relevance: Documents severe drought-driven livestock mortality in the Somali region and Borana zone.

28. **Pastoralists' and agro-pastoralists' livelihood resilience to climate change-induced risks in the Borana zone, south Ethiopia: Using resilience index measurement approach** (2023). Daniel Assefa Tofu, C. Fana, Tegegn Dilbato, et al.. *Pastoralism*. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13570-022-00263-3
   Relevance: Measures low livelihood resilience of Borana pastoralists to recurrent drought, supplying vulnerability indicators.

29. **Integration of satellite data for predicting crop yields in Eastern Ethiopia using machine learning** (2025). Jemal Abate, Araba Aman, Dima Adem. *Scientific Reports*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-00810-z
   Relevance: A Random Forest crop yield prediction model for Eastern Ethiopia integrating satellite-derived environmental data, the closest regional precedent to the thesis's multimodal approach.


## 2. Drought Monitoring and Prediction with Deep Learning and Remote Sensing

30. **Construction of a drought monitoring model using deep learning based on multi-source remote sensing data** (2019). R. Shen, Anqi Huang, Bolun Li, et al.. *International Journal of Applied Earth Observation and Geoinformation*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2019.03.006
   Relevance: Canonical multi-source (MODIS, TRMM) deep learning drought monitoring model validated against SPEI.

31. **Deep Learning for Monitoring Agricultural Drought in South Asia Using Remote Sensing Data** (2021). Foyez Ahmed Prodhan, Jiahua Zhang, Fengmei Yao, et al.. *Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13091715
   Relevance: Deep feedforward neural network predicting a soil moisture deficit index from precipitation, vegetation, and soil factors.

32. **Construction of an Integrated Drought Monitoring Model Based on Deep Learning Algorithms** (2023). Yonghong Zhang, Donglin Xie, Wei Tian, et al.. *Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030667
   Relevance: ConvLSTM-based composite drought index from MODIS and CHIRPS inputs benchmarked against multi-scale SPEI and SPI.

33. **Evaluating established deep learning methods in constructing integrated remote sensing drought index: A case study in China** (2023). Zhenheng Xu, Hao Sun, Tian Zhang, et al.. *Agricultural Water Management*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agwat.2023.108405
   Relevance: Systematic comparison of four deep architectures (EEDNN, 1D-CNN, GRU, self-attention) versus tree-based ML for building an integrated drought index.

34. **Leveraging multisource data for accurate agricultural drought monitoring: A hybrid deep learning model** (2024). Xin Xiao, Wenting Ming, Xuan Luo, et al.. *Agricultural Water Management*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agwat.2024.108692
   Relevance: Hybrid CNN-Random Forest model fusing MODIS, GLDAS, CHIRPS, and DEM data to reproduce SPEI-3 in a data-scarce mountainous region.

35. **An improved SPEI drought forecasting approach using the long short-term memory neural network.** (2021). Abhirup Dikshit, B. Pradhan, A. Huete. *Journal of Environmental Management*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2021.111979
   Relevance: Widely cited LSTM forecasting of SPEI at multiple time scales against RF and ANN baselines.

36. **Estimation of SPEI Meteorological Drought Using Machine Learning Algorithms** (2021). A. Mokhtar, Mohammadnabi Jalali, Hongming He, et al.. *IEEE Access*. https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2021.3074305
   Relevance: RF, XGBoost, CNN, and LSTM estimation of SPEI-3 and SPEI-6 under multiple climate-input scenarios.

37. **Deep learning based drought assessment and prediction framework** (2020). Amandeep Kaur, S. Sood. *Ecological Informatics*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoinf.2020.101067
   Relevance: End-to-end DNN framework for drought severity assessment and SVR-based prediction.

38. **Nationwide Prediction of Drought Conditions in Iran Based on Remote Sensing Data** (2013). Mahdi Jalili, Joobin Gharibshah, Seyed Morsal Ghavami, et al.. *IEEE Transactions on Computers*. https://doi.org/10.1109/tc.2013.118
   Relevance: Foundational ANN prediction of SPI from satellite-derived NDVI, VCI, and TCI features.

39. **Droughts of the Late 1980s in the United States as Derived from NOAA Polar-Orbiting Satellite Data** (1995). Felix Kogan. *Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society*. https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0477(1995)076<0655:dotlit>2.0.co;2
   Relevance: Seminal derivation of the Vegetation Condition Index (VCI) from AVHRR data.

40. **Drought Monitoring with NDVI-Based Standardized Vegetation Index** (2002). Albert J. Peters, Elizabeth A. Walter-Shea, Lei Ji, et al.. *Photogrammetric Engineering & Remote Sensing (as indexed)*. No DOI indexed
   Relevance: Foundational Standardized Vegetation Index method that standardizes NDVI anomalies by season.

41. **Agricultural drought forecasting using satellite images, climate indices and artificial neural network** (2011). Ahmad Fatehi Marj, A.M.J. Meijerink. *International Journal of Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.1080/01431161.2011.575896
   Relevance: Early ANN forecasting of NDVI from satellite imagery and climate oscillation indices.

42. **A remote sensing and artificial neural network-based integrated agricultural drought index: Index development and applications** (2019). Xianfeng Liu, Xiufang Zhu, Qiang Zhang, et al.. *CATENA*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.catena.2019.104394
   Relevance: Back-propagation neural network integrating NDVI, LST, precipitation, and soil variables into an integrated drought index validated against SPI-3/SPEI-3.

43. **Application of Artificial Neural Networks in Forecasting a Standardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index for the Upper Blue Nile Basin** (2020). Getachew Mulualem, Yuei-An Liou. *Water*. https://doi.org/10.3390/w12030643
   Relevance: ANN forecasting of SPEI with climate and sea-surface-temperature predictors in the Ethiopian Upper Blue Nile basin.

44. **Drought Spatial Object Prediction Approach using Artificial Neural Network** (2015). Getachew Berhan, Tsegaye Tadesse. *Geoinformatics & Geostatistics: An Overview*. https://doi.org/10.4172/2327-4581.1000132
   Relevance: Ethiopia-specific ANN drought prediction from multisensor satellite attributes including SDNDVI and SPI.

45. **Enhanced Drought Prediction in Ethiopia: A Deep Learning Approach** (2026). A. Mamuye, Mengistu Belete, Redet Getachew, et al.. *Working paper (as indexed)*. https://doi.org/10.55158/deepcfwp11
   Relevance: Recent deep learning drought early-warning framework for Ethiopia's arid lowlands using remote sensing and climate metrics.

46. **Deep Learning for Drought and Vegetation Health Modelling: Demonstrating the utility of an Entity-Aware LSTM** (2020). Thomas Lees, Gabriel Tseng, Steven Reece, et al.. *EGU General Assembly abstract*. https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu2020-8173
   Relevance: Entity-aware LSTM forecasting satellite-derived VCI in Kenya using ERA5 and CHIRPS data.

47. **Benchmarking Vegetation Forecasts for Drought Early Warning in Eastern Africa** (2026). Chloe Hopling, Claire Robin, Vitus Benson, et al.. *EGU General Assembly abstract*. https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu26-20907
   Relevance: Benchmarks autoregressive, ConvLSTM, and transformer models for NDVI/VCI forecasting for drought early warning in the Greater Horn of Africa.

48. **Evaluation of machine learning approaches for large-scale agricultural drought forecasts to improve monitoring and preparedness in Brazil** (2025). J. W. Gallear, Marcelo Valadares Galdos, Marcelo Zeri, et al.. *Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences*. https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-25-1521-2025
   Relevance: Gradient-boosting forecasts of the Vegetation Health Index with SPEI as drought-stress indicator.

49. **Spatio-temporal drought dynamics in Niamey District, Niger (2005-2022): multi-index deep learning approach leveraging multi-sensor satellite and climate datasets** (2026). Mohamed Adou Sidi Almouctar, Jérôme Chenal, El Bachir Diop, et al.. *Scientific Reports*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-61854-3
   Relevance: Multi-sensor LSTM pipeline monitoring four drought indices (SPI, SMI, PDSI, VHI) from Landsat, Sentinel, MODIS, and CHIRPS.

50. **DroughtSet: Understanding Drought Through Spatial-Temporal Learning** (2024). Xuwei Tan, Qian Zhao, Yanlan Liu, et al.. *arXiv (preprint)*. https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2412.15075
   Relevance: Open benchmark dataset and spatial-temporal model (SPDrought) predicting three drought indices from remote sensing and reanalysis features.

51. **Focal-TSMP: deep learning for vegetation health prediction and agricultural drought assessment from a regional climate simulation** (2024). Mohamad Hakam Shams Eddin, Jüergen Gall. *Geoscientific Model Development*. https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-17-2987-2024
   Relevance: Deep learning generation of NDVI anomaly, VCI, TCI, and VHI maps for agricultural drought assessment.

52. **Enhanced prediction of vegetation responses to extreme drought using deep learning and Earth observation data** (2024). Klaus-Rudolf Kladny, Marco Milanta, Oto Mraz, et al.. *Ecological Informatics*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoinf.2024.102474
   Relevance: ConvLSTM forecasting of vegetation greenness anomalies under extreme drought from Earth observation time series.

53. **Deep learning the droughts: Spatiotemporal monitoring of protracted dry conditions in Queensland, Australia** (2026). Charles Joseph, Qingxia (Jenny) Wang, Shahbaz Mushtaq, et al.. *The Science of The Total Environment*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2026.181564
   Relevance: CNN-LSTM with multi-head attention linking SPI drought events to NDVI recovery dynamics.

54. **Drought Prediction using Deep Learning Framework with Multi-sensor Satellite Images** (2025). Aayushi Dhote, Dev Sharma, Harsh Patel, et al.. *2025 IEEE ACROSET*. https://doi.org/10.1109/acroset66531.2025.11281354
   Relevance: Spatio-temporal graph neural network versus LSTM on fused Landsat-Sentinel, MODIS, and Sentinel-1 data.

55. **Spatio-Temporal Deep Learning Models for Forecasting Agricultural Drought in Rain-Fed Regions** (2025). V. Chandra Shekhar Rao, Voore Subrahmanyam, A Manjula, et al.. *International Journal of Environmental Sciences*. No DOI indexed
   Relevance: Comparative evaluation of LSTM, CNN-LSTM, ConvLSTM, transformer, and GNN architectures using SPI/SPEI/PDSI and NDVI/VCI inputs in rain-fed agriculture.

56. **A Systematic Review of Spatiotemporal Deep Learning Approaches for Drought Monitoring and Forecasting using Remote Sensing Data** (2026). Ritu Khandelwal, Hemlata Goyal, Rajveer Singh Shekhawat. *Recent Advances in Computer Science and Communications*. https://doi.org/10.2174/0126662558510620260916054954
   Relevance: Systematic review of drought indices and spatio-temporal ML/DL models on remote sensing data.

57. **Agricultural Drought Forecasting Using NDVI, VCI and Artificial Neural Network** (2023). Shubhangi Wankhede, Leisa Armstrong, Amiya Kumar Tripathy. *IGARSS 2023*. https://doi.org/10.1109/ingarss59135.2023.10490414
   Relevance: ANN forecasting of agricultural drought severity from NDVI and VCI combined with meteorological parameters.


## 3. Satellite Data Products: Soil Moisture, Rainfall, Vegetation and Thermal Indices

58. **The Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) Mission** (2010). Dara Entekhabi, E. G. Njoku, Peggy O'Neill, et al.. *Proceedings of the IEEE*. https://doi.org/10.1109/jproc.2010.2043918
   Relevance: Foundational mission paper describing the SMAP L-band radar/radiometer design and products.

59. **Agricultural Drought Monitoring via the Assimilation of SMAP Soil Moisture Retrievals Into a Global Soil Water Balance Model** (2020). I. Mladenova, J. Bolten, W. Crow, et al.. *Frontiers in Big Data*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fdata.2020.00010
   Relevance: Assimilated SMAP soil moisture improves root-zone drought monitoring, benchmarked against CHIRPS rainfall and NDVI.

60. **Evaluating the Operational Application of SMAP for Global Agricultural Drought Monitoring** (2019). I. E. Mladenova, John Bolten, Wade T. Crow, et al.. *IEEE JSTARS*. https://doi.org/10.1109/jstars.2019.2923555
   Relevance: Operational-scale assessment of SMAP assimilation for agricultural drought with NDVI-correlation validation.

61. **SMAP Soil Moisture Change as an Indicator of Drought Conditions** (2018). Eswar Rajasekaran, Narendra N. Das, Calvin Poulsen, et al.. *Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs10050788
   Relevance: Temporal change in SMAP soil moisture detects drought intensification.

62. **Developing a drought-monitoring index for the contiguous US using SMAP** (2018). S. Sadri, E. Wood, M. Pan. *Hydrology and Earth System Sciences*. https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-22-6611-2018
   Relevance: Near-surface and root-zone SMAP drought indices via beta-distribution percentiles.

63. **Standardized Soil Moisture Index for Drought Monitoring Based on Soil Moisture Active Passive Observations and 36 Years of North American Land Data Assimilation System Data** (2018). Yaping Xu, Lei Wang, Kenton Ross, et al.. *Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs10020301
   Relevance: Fuses SMAP soil moisture with long-term land data assimilation records into a standardized drought warning index.

64. **Satellite Soil Moisture for Agricultural Drought Monitoring: Assessment of SMAP-Derived Soil Water Deficit Index in Xiang River Basin, China** (2019). Qian Zhu, Yulin Luo, Yue-Ping Xu, et al.. *Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11030362
   Relevance: Validates the SMAP-derived Soil Water Deficit Index in a data-scarce agricultural basin.

65. **A global near-real-time soil moisture index monitor for food security using integrated SMOS and SMAP** (2020). S. Sadri, M. Pan, Y. Wada, et al.. *Remote Sensing of Environment*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2020.111864
   Relevance: Near-real-time global soil moisture drought monitor for food security merging SMOS and SMAP.

66. **Evaluating Satellite Soil Moisture Datasets for Drought Monitoring in Australia and the South-West Pacific** (2022). Jessica Bhardwaj, Yuriy Kuleshov, Zhi-Weng Chua, et al.. *Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14163971
   Relevance: Intercompares SMAP, SMOS, AMSR-2, ASCAT and SMOPS products for agrometeorological drought.

67. **Leveraging the Google Earth Engine for Drought Assessment Using Global Soil Moisture Data** (2018). Nazmus Sazib, I. E. Mladenova, John Bolten. *Remote Sensing*. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs10081265
   Relevance: Assesses soil moisture, NDVI and SPI drought indicators over drought-prone Ethiopia and South Africa on Google Earth Engine.

68. **Global Flash Drought Monitoring Using Surface Soil Moisture** (2021). Vinit Sehgal, Nandita Gaur, Binayak P. Mohanty. *Water Resources Research*. https://doi.org/10.1029/2021wr029901
   Relevance: SMAP-based flash drought stress index with lead-time skill for vegetation health.

69. **Validation of the CHIRPS satellite rainfall estimates over eastern Africa** (2018). Tufa Dinku, Chris Funk, Pete Peterson, et al.. *Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society*. https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3244
   Relevance: The reference validation of CHIRPS over East Africa.

70. **Validation of new satellite rainfall products over the Upper Blue Nile Basin, Ethiopia** (2018). Getachew Tesfaye Ayehu, Tsegaye Tadesse, Berhan Gessesse, Tufa Dinku. *Atmospheric Measurement Techniques*. https://doi.org/10.5194/amt-11-1921-2018
   Relevance: Ethiopia-specific evaluation showing CHIRPS outperforms TAMSAT3 and ARC2.

71. **Performance Evaluation and Comparison of Satellite-Derived Rainfall Datasets over the Ziway Lake Basin, Ethiopia** (2021). Aster Tesfaye Hordofa, Olkeba Tolessa Leta, Tena Alamirew, et al.. *Climate*. https://doi.org/10.3390/cli9070113
   Relevance: Compares GPM-IMERG runs against CHIRPS over an Ethiopian basin.

72. **Validation of Satellite Rainfall Estimates over Equatorial East Africa** (2021). Simon Ageet, Andreas H. Fink, Marlon Maranan, et al.. *Journal of Hydrometeorology*. https://doi.org/10.1175/jhm-d-21-0145.1
   Relevance: Multiscale evaluation of IMERG, CHIRPS and MSWEP in equatorial East Africa including extreme-event detection.

73. **Validation of IMERG Precipitation in Africa** (2017). Amin Dezfuli, Charles Ichoku, George J. Huffman, et al.. *Journal of Hydrometeorology*. https://doi.org/10.1175/jhm-d-17-0139.1
   Relevance: Continent-scale IMERG validation against gauge data with regional skill mapping.

74. **Accuracy of satellite and reanalysis rainfall estimates over Africa: A multi-scale assessment of eight products for continental applications** (2023). Kirubel Mekonnen, Naga Manohar Velpuri, Mansoor Leh, et al.. *Journal of Hydrology: Regional Studies*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrh.2023.101514
   Relevance: Identifies CHIRPS as most reliable in Eastern Africa for drought applications.
